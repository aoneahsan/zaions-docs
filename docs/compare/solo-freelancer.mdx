# Coming from a solo freelancer

_Last checked November 8, 2026._

One person, hired directly, is often the cheapest way to get a small job done. There's no company around them
to pay for, and a good one starts quickly.

## Jobs one person covers

A bug in software you already have. A landing page. A logo. A small script. If one skill covers the job, you
can describe the finish line in two sentences, and you have the time to check what comes back, hire the
freelancer. One good person may be all you need.

## When the job needs more than one skill

Most products do. Here's where the two routes split:

- **Skills.** A freelancer brings whatever that person does. With us, design, code, testing, store graphics and
  video sit under one plan.
- **Testing.** With one person, it's up to them. With us, it's in every [milestone](../how-a-project-runs.mdx).
- **Progress.** Messages, versus a link to the working build at every milestone.
- **The end.** Whatever they hand over, versus the [handover](../handover.mdx) checklist, where every item is
  listed by name.

## Taking over from your freelancer

1. Before anything else, ask them for the repository, the accounts and the passwords, and make sure each one is
   in your name.
2. Write a [brief](../brief.mdx) that says what already exists and what comes next.
3. We estimate the work from where the code stands today, not from zero.
